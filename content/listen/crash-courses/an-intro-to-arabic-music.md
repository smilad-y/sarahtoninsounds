There's a good chance you've already listened to Arabic music without even realizing it.

Arabic music has been sampled in hip-hop, pop, electronic music, and just about everything in between. Some of those melodies you've had stuck in your head actually came from songs recorded decades ago. And guess what? A few of the originals are right here in this playlist.

Take Abdel Halim Hafez's "Khosara," for example. If you've ever listened to Jay-Z's "Big Pimpin'," you've already heard it. And that's just one example of how far these sounds have traveled.

Growing up Egyptian, Arabic music was always around me. From the classics to the pop songs I grew up listening to, it's a sound that's always felt familiar. But the older I get, the more I find myself going back and discovering just how much music there is to explore.

And there's A LOT.

You can't really talk about Arabic music without mentioning Umm Kulthum and Fairuz. Their voices weren't just the soundtrack to people's lives. They became part of their history, politics, and identity. Umm Kulthum's music brought people together across Egypt and the Arab world, becoming a symbol of Egyptian pride and Arab unity. Fairuz's voice became deeply connected to Lebanon, carrying a sense of home, nostalgia, and belonging for generations of listeners. Their music has lived through wars, political movements, and decades of change, and their influence is still felt today.

And that's something I love about Arabic music. It's not just about the songs themselves. It's about the memories, the people, and the history attached to them.

But Arabic music isn't just one sound. You've got the dramatic, larger-than-life classics, the funky stuff, the love songs, the pop hits, and newer artists finding their own ways to bring those influences into their music.

For this playlist, I put together a mix of Arabic classics and modern favorites, starting with the older recordings and working my way toward the newer stuff. Think of it as a little trip through the years, with some familiar melodies and hopefully a few new favorites along the way.

Whether you grew up listening to Arabic music, recognize a sample or two, or have absolutely no idea where to start, there's something here for you.

And if you find yourself recognizing a melody, go ahead and look up where you've heard it before. You might be surprised.

Happy listening!

— Sarah
