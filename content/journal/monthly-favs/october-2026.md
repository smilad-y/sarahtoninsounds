reflection: >-
  October was a packed month! Right at the start, I finally got to cross
  something off my concert bucket list: seeing one of my favorite artists,
  Hayley Williams, live for the first time. Or Ms. Williams, if ya nasty!


  I saw her at the Hollywood Bowl, and I think one little moment from that night sums up the experience better than I could otherwise explain it. A stranger standing next to me noticed tears rolling down my face, gently put her hand on my shoulder, and asked if I was alright.


  I was more than alright. I felt at peace. Dancing, of course, but at peace. Watching one of my favorite artists sing her heart out, call for a free Palestine, and getting to share it all with my best friend.


  Later in the month, I headed to Massachusetts for one of my high school best friends' weddings. It turned into a little reunion with friends I've known since we were kids, and it's so fun how being together brings out that younger version of ourselves. The same girls I got ready with for prom are now the girls I'm getting ready with for a wedding. The wedding was gorgeous, and we even got to explore a bit of Maine and New Hampshire while we were there.


  Then, because apparently I hadn't traveled enough, I booked a last-minute flight to New York to spend time with my mom and sister and catch up with some college friends. East Coast fall really is something else. I understand why so many rom-coms are set there in the fall.


  And somehow, I still had enough energy left for Halloween, where I dressed up as a pool shark. A pretty perfect ending to a month full of music, travel, friends, family, and lots of dressing up.

    note: >-
      Everything about “Jamie” has me entranced. I’ve had it on repeat pretty
      much every day since Remi Wolf’s new album, Mud, came out. It’s sultry and
      warm. She starts off practically talking over these sliding guitars,
      sounding a little ungrounded and a little sarcastic. When she says “type
      shit” in the second verse, you can almost picture the little nod that goes
      along with it.


      Then she starts singing, and it’s like she just lets go. Her voice opens up as the song builds, and the way she goes from basically talking to these huge vocal moments is what keeps bringing me back. I want to sway and step along to the beat one minute, and the next I’m completely caught up in her voice, pretending I’m performing the song myself. I mean, when she sings “Jamie” after the second verse, I physically have to bend over like singers do when they’re belting out a note with their entire body. I’m putting my whole self into it, and I’m not even the one singing. And I love, love, love the outro after the bridge.


      There’s also so much going on in the story she’s telling. Regret, attachment, addiction, nostalgia, even a little implied murder. It’s messy, and she sounds almost desperate about Jamie the longer the song goes. But by the outro, we’re suddenly imagining this little life together. Hot yoga, going to get Zyns, all these random things that somehow feel romantic when you’re young and stupidly into someone. Like, I would love nothing more than to go get Zyns with your stupid ass.


      I’ve always had a soft spot for songs about unrequited love, and the part of “Jamie” I relate to most is getting a little carried away imagining what life together could look like. Those little fantasies are so sweet, even with the sadness underneath them.

    note: >-
      always listen to Once Upon a Summertime when I’m in New York, and I was
      there for part of October, so it was back in rotation. Fall in New York,
      the most beautiful trees, autumnal colors everywhere, and Blossom Dearie
      in my headphones. It doesn’t get much better than that.


      I’ve always loved this album. It’s charming and playful, but melancholic at the same time, which is something I’m clearly drawn to (see: “Jamie”). New York is so stimulating. Everyone’s doing something, everyone’s in a hurry. But put this album on against that backdrop and suddenly everything feels unhurried and light. Feathery, even. I love that juxtaposition.


      “Manhattan” is especially dear to me because it was the song that introduced me to Blossom Dearie many moons ago. It’s also the song that first made me romanticize the idea of strolling through Central Park in the fall. So it’s an essential part of my New York soundtrack. I love listening to her sing about the city while I’m actually wandering through it.


      Anyway, I adore Blossom Dearie, and this album has become one of my favorites for moseying around the park and people-watching.
